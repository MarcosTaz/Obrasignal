## Material negative evidence

Rodtec replied that it did not clearly understand the offer and, more importantly, that the company does not look for contracts/opportunities through portals and therefore does not believe the offer applies to it.

This is evidence against using a broad segment label such as "technical installations companies" as sufficient ICP qualification. The workflow problem only exists strongly where the target actually performs recurring opportunity discovery/triage.

## Current interpretation

The current proposition has two separately testable risks:

1. **Problem-presence risk** — some technically relevant contractors do not use tender/procurement portals, so they have little or no recurring portal-triage pain.
2. **Message-clarity risk** — at least one recipient explicitly reported not understanding the offer well.

One negative reply is not sufficient to reject the whole wedge, but it is sufficient to tighten qualification and to avoid repeating the same broad targeting logic unchanged.

## Next ICP qualification rule

For the next controlled cohort, require evidence of the workflow before outreach. A prospect should satisfy at least one strong indicator such as:

- explicit participation in public procurement / public works tenders;
- a tenders, procurement, bidding, estimating, proposals or public-contracts role/function;
- public evidence of awarded public contracts or recurring tender participation;
- explicit use of procurement/tender portals;
- a business model in which identifying and triaging external project opportunities is a recurring commercial activity.

Technical fit alone is no longer enough.

## Next problem statement to test

Prefer a concrete workflow proposition over a generic opportunity-discovery proposition:

> Reduce the time spent finding and triaging public-work opportunities your company could actually bid, while showing why each opportunity fits and what requires attention next.

The key proof question is not whether companies "like opportunities". It is whether the current discovery/triage workflow is frequent and costly enough that a filtered, explainable shortlist creates measurable value.

## Next commercial experiment — prepared, not sent

Target a small cohort of portal-active public-works contractors / technical installers with verified tender participation. Change one meaningful variable: qualification by demonstrated procurement workflow. Keep the offer deliberately assisted and measure:

- reply;
- confirmation that opportunity discovery/triage is a real recurring workflow;
- current workaround and time burden;
- willingness to test with the company's own profile;
- first useful shortlist delivered;
- whether the recipient acts on or saves at least one surfaced opportunity;
- willingness to continue and willingness to pay.

No outbound message is authorized by this artifact. Customer communication remains an explicit real-world action and must use the appropriate authorization boundary.
