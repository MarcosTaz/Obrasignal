The problem

Suppliers often absorb change costs because the proof is scattered.

The commercial basis may sit in a PO, the instruction in an email, the engineering hours in another system and the mitigation in a spreadsheet. Certactum is designed to reconstruct that chain while preserving the distinction between documented cost, contractual basis and reviewed recovery position.

Baseline

What was originally agreed, priced, scoped or committed?

Customer-caused change

What changed, when, by whose instruction, and what evidence supports that fact?

Incremental economics

What documented cost remained after prior payment, avoided cost, reuse, resale and other mitigation?

Assisted recovery workflow

Documents → facts → causation → economics → human review → dossier.

01

Rapid recovery scan

Professional proof dossier

Receive a reviewable package with evidence status, calculation lineage, chronology, truth boundaries and next-action recommendations — including documented NO CLAIM / NEEDS REVIEW outcomes where appropriate.

Initial wedge

Start with customer-directed engineering and change cost.

The first Certactum case type is NRE / change-driven incremental cost for industrial suppliers. The same evidence-first architecture can support adjacent recovery categories as they are validated.

Strong starting signals

  • A customer instruction, revision, cancellation or scope change can be identified.
  • There is a pre-change commercial or technical baseline.
  • Incremental engineering, tooling, material, freight or disruption cost can be documented.
  • Prior payment and mitigation can be reconciled.

Cases requiring more evidence

  • A cost exists, but no customer-caused event can yet be supported.
  • A spreadsheet estimate lacks its underlying documentary trail.
  • The amount has not yet reconciled reuse, resale, prior recovery or avoided cost.
  • Material contractual questions still require specialist review.
Certactum truth: every conclusion remains tied to its underlying evidence, chronology and calculation lineage. The dossier preserves what is established, what still requires review and what the available evidence does not support.
