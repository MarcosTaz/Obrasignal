## Pilot target

Portugal-based construction/electrical/AVAC contractor with demonstrated recurring participation in public procurement and an internal need to discover or triage opportunities before proposal preparation.

## Customer problem to validate

The company spends recurring time identifying public-work opportunities, discarding irrelevant notices, checking fit and deciding which opportunities deserve deeper bid analysis.

This problem is not assumed merely because a company has won public contracts. The pilot begins by confirming whether the workflow exists and is painful enough to change.

## Assisted pilot promise

For a bounded pilot period, ObraSignal will:

1. configure the company's real capability/profile constraints;
2. monitor traceable public opportunity sources already supported by the product;
3. surface a small explainable shortlist instead of a generic feed;
4. show why each opportunity fits or why confidence is limited;
5. let the company save and track opportunities through its workflow;
6. support the pilot through the documented assisted-support route;
7. review actual usage and usefulness with the customer.

No promise is made that the customer will win a contract, save a specific amount, or discover opportunities that did not otherwise exist.

## Required customer inputs

- company name and main activities;
- work categories / CPV families if known;
- geography accepted;
- typical contract-size range;
- key exclusions;
- current opportunity-discovery method;
- role/person who performs first triage;
- approximate current frequency/time burden, if measurable.

## First value

Candidate First Value Event:

**At least one previously untriaged current opportunity is surfaced with traceable source evidence and the customer judges it useful enough to investigate, save or assign.**

This becomes real first-value evidence only when observed with a real customer.

## Repeat value

Repeat value is not “the customer logged in again.” The pilot tests whether ObraSignal repeatedly reduces discovery/triage work or repeatedly surfaces useful current opportunities across separate work cycles.

Measure:
- number of shortlist cycles delivered;
- opportunities opened;
- opportunities judged irrelevant;
- opportunities saved/investigated/assigned;
- return after notification or new shortlist;
- customer-reported replacement or reduction of existing triage work.

## Pilot success criteria

A pilot is successful only if evidence supports multiple dimensions:

- customer confirms the discovery/triage problem is real and recurring;
- profile can be configured without excessive setup burden;
- source/relevance truth is understandable and trusted;
- at least one First Value Event occurs;
- the customer returns for another shortlist/workflow cycle;
- no P0 security/tenant/customer-truth defect occurs;
- support burden is measurable and manageable;
- the customer states a concrete reason to continue;
- willingness to pay is tested explicitly.

## Pricing experiment

Current monthly pricing ideas remain hypotheses, not validated truth.

For the first workflow-qualified pilots, test willingness to pay separately from technical usefulness. Possible experiment sequence:

1. confirm problem and willingness to test before quoting a subscription;
2. run a tightly bounded assisted sample/pilot where appropriate;
3. after first/repeat value, ask what commercial model the customer would accept;
4. test a concrete paid continuation offer rather than asking only “what would you pay?”;
5. record objection: price / trust / frequency / data / incumbent process / no budget / no problem.

Do not infer €99/month or any other price is validated until real purchase behavior supports it.
