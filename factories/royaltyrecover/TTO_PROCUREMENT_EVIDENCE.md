# RoyaltyRecover — TTO Procurement & Audit Economics Evidence

Date: 2026-08-29
Status: MATERIAL BEACHHEAD EVIDENCE

## University of Kentucky — unusually strong public case

University of Kentucky procurement records list a Royalty Compliance Audit contract, `UK-2216-22`, awarded to InvotexIP, LLC, with contract coverage shown through 2027 in the University's current-contract listing.

Sources:
- https://purchasing.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/PriceContractsbyCommodityAug2023_0.pdf
- https://purchasing.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2021-12/uk-2216-22qanda.pdf

The RFP Q&A states:
- 170+ active license agreements were in place;
- an audit target had already been selected for the immediate engagement;
- additional audits could be requested over the contract life;
- whether the University had a royalty-audit work plan covering all contracts was answered `unknown`;
- each audit would have its own timeframe;
- the selected licensee had already been chosen rather than asking the vendor to risk-rank the whole portfolio.

This is direct evidence for the gap RoyaltyRecover is testing: a sizeable TTO portfolio can procure formal audit capability while still lacking an explicit portfolio-wide prioritization workflow.

## Audit unit economics — disclosed real case

A University of Kentucky Board of Trustees / Finance Committee presentation reports a completed InvotexIP audit of the Pall Technology license. The license had generated $4,524,312 for UKRF over its life before the audit. The audit team visited the licensee's European manufacturing plant and found patented products sold but omitted from royalty calculations.

The University reported:
- additional royalty payment from the audit: **$2,068,138**;
- audit cost: **$31,765.84**.

Source:
- https://www.uky.edu/trustees/sites/www.uky.edu.trustees/files/BOT%20Finance%20Committee%20Research%20VPR%2002%202024_draftlc.pdf

Simple observed economics for this one disclosed audit:
- incremental royalty / audit cost ≈ **65.1x**;
- audit cost / incremental royalty ≈ **1.54%**.

These are case-specific observed ratios, not expected portfolio averages.

## Product consequence

This sharpens the economic job of the initial product:

**Do not replace the $30k-class formal audit. Improve the decision about where to spend it.**

For a TTO with 170+ active agreements, auditing every licensee at similar cost would be economically and operationally unrealistic. A low-friction desk layer that identifies:
- supported anomalies;
- missing evidence;
- high-value streams;
- never-audited/high-risk agreements;
- closing lookback windows;
- cases where the licensee appears correct;
can create value even when a qualified audit firm remains essential for the field audit.

## Pricing implication

This evidence does NOT establish a market-wide royalty audit price, but it gives one credible formal-audit benchmark around $31.8k. RoyaltyRecover's proposed scan bands should therefore be tested as a fraction of avoided/more-targeted formal-audit spend rather than as generic SaaS pricing.

A commercially sensible experiment remains:
- bounded small portfolio triage below formal-audit cost;
- portfolio prioritization priced to be clearly cheaper than even one unnecessary formal audit;
- formal audit performed by partner when justified;
- recurring monitoring only after the first portfolio baseline is established.

The current EUR 1.5k–12k scan bands remain hypotheses, but this public case substantially strengthens the economic plausibility of a scan priced below one formal audit.

## UC system evidence reinforces portfolio triage

The University of California's 2023 proposed royalty-audit identification approach explicitly calls for ongoing risk-based monitoring to identify strong audit candidates and notes that a centralized approach could gain economies of scale and consistency by screening a consolidated, normalized dataset. It also discusses self-audit and audit-reimbursement provisions to defray audit costs.

Source:
- https://regents.universityofcalifornia.edu/regmeet/july23/s1attach1.pdf

This is especially important: the proposed RoyaltyRecover operating model is not inventing a workflow universities do not want. Major university systems already articulate the need for centralized, risk-based candidate identification. The differentiation challenge is execution economics and productization, not problem discovery.
