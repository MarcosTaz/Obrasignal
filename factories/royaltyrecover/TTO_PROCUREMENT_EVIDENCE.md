# RoyaltyRecover — TTO Procurement & Audit Economics Evidence

Date: 2026-08-29
Status: MATERIAL BEACHHEAD EVIDENCE

## University of Kentucky — unusually strong public case

University of Kentucky procurement records list a Royalty Compliance Audit contract, `UK-2216-22`, awarded to InvotexIP, LLC, with contract coverage shown through 2027 in the University's current-contract listing.

Sources:
- https://purchasing.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/PriceContractsbyCommodityAug2023_0.pdf
- https://purchasing.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2021-12/uk-2216-22qanda.pdf

The RFP Q&A states:
- 170+ active license agreements were in place;
- an audit target had already been selected for the immediate engagement;
- additional audits could be requested over the contract life;
- whether the University had a royalty-audit work plan covering all contracts was answered `unknown`;
- each audit would have its own timeframe;
- the selected licensee had already been chosen rather than asking the vendor to risk-rank the whole portfolio.

This is direct evidence for the gap RoyaltyRecover is testing: a sizeable TTO portfolio can procure formal audit capability while still lacking an explicit portfolio-wide prioritization workflow.

## Kentucky audit unit economics — disclosed real case

A University of Kentucky Board of Trustees / Finance Committee presentation reports a completed InvotexIP audit of the Pall Technology license. The license had generated $4,524,312 for UKRF over its life before the audit. The audit team visited the licensee's European manufacturing plant and found patented products sold but omitted from royalty calculations.

The University reported:
- additional royalty payment from the audit: **$2,068,138**;
- audit cost: **$31,765.84**.

Source:
- https://www.uky.edu/trustees/sites/www.uky.edu.trustees/files/BOT%20Finance%20Committee%20Research%20VPR%2002%202024_draftlc.pdf

Simple observed economics for this one disclosed audit:
- incremental royalty / audit cost ≈ **65.1x**;
- audit cost / incremental royalty ≈ **1.54%**.

These are case-specific observed ratios, not expected portfolio averages.

## University of California — published screening and audit economics

UC's June 2023 proposed Royalty Audit Identification Approach gives unusually useful operating benchmarks:
- risk-based ongoing monitoring is defined as the first step for identifying strong audit candidates;
- UC found most campuses did not conduct systematic monitoring because available resources were insufficient;
- campuses generally supported centralized resourced monitoring, combined with local knowledge;
- a desk audit compares available information to license obligations and can end in close/no action, a request for information, or invocation of a full audit;
- **desk audits average approximately $3,000 each**;
- based on outside-consultant experience, **approximately 50% of desk audits identify concerns meriting transition to full royalty audits**;
- **full audits average approximately $40,000–$60,000**;
- full-audit costs may be reimbursed when a contract's audit-reimbursement threshold is met;
- UC reports approximately $6.6m of underpayment identified over 14 years at net audit cost of about $591k, even before a coordinated identification process.

Source:
- https://regents.universityofcalifornia.edu/regmeet/july23/s1attach1.pdf

This evidence is critical because it separates three economic layers that RoyaltyRecover should not blur:

1. **Portfolio pre-screen / recoverability triage** — RoyaltyRecover's intended entry wedge.
2. **Desk audit** — an existing approximately-$3k-per-licensee professional activity in UC's benchmark.
3. **Full audit** — an existing approximately-$40k–$60k activity, often involving licensee books, product-scope work and possibly outside counsel.

## Product consequence

RoyaltyRecover should not initially compete head-on as a cheaper full audit or merely automate a $3k desk audit one licensee at a time.

The stronger economic job is:

**SCREEN MANY LICENSEES CHEAPLY ENOUGH TO DECIDE WHICH $3K DESK REVIEWS AND $40K–$60K FULL AUDITS ARE WORTH BUYING BEFORE CONTRACTUAL WINDOWS CLOSE.**

That means the primary product metric is not just finding count. It is:
- cost per licensee screened;
- percentage of portfolio deterministically cleared as no-current-recovery-signal;
- percentage requiring missing data;
- percentage elevated to professional desk review;
- percentage elevated to formal audit;
- false-positive rate against clean controls;
- elapsed time to triage a portfolio;
- avoided unnecessary desk/full audit spend;
- recoveries ultimately attributable to cases selected by the triage.

## Pricing implication

The earlier broad EUR 1.5k–12k scan bands need refinement. UC's $3k average desk-audit figure creates a useful commercial ceiling for a *single-licensee* pre-audit screen: RoyaltyRecover cannot plausibly charge close to a professional desk audit while delivering less professional judgment.

The testable pricing architecture should therefore emphasize portfolio economics:
- a bounded multi-licensee baseline scan priced below the cost of manually desk-auditing the same portfolio;
- a target per-licensee screening cost materially below ~$3k, ideally an order of magnitude lower at portfolio scale once onboarding is normalized;
- professional desk/full-audit fees remain separate and partner-delivered where needed;
- recurring monitoring becomes attractive only after the portfolio baseline is created.

No exact price is frozen yet. The public evidence validates the economic shape, not customer willingness to pay a particular RoyaltyRecover price.

## Strategic consequence for Gate 3

UC's proposed methodology proves the workflow demand but also makes the differentiation test harder: centralized risk screening, questionnaires, desk audits and escalation are already known best practice. Therefore RoyaltyRecover cannot claim novelty from the workflow itself.

The remaining defensible differentiators must be proven in execution:
- near-zero migration/onboarding burden;
- much lower cost per agreement screened;
- deterministic evidence lineage and explicit no-recovery outcomes;
- automated staleness/version controls that prevent old rules/data from silently supporting findings;
- deadline-aware portfolio prioritization;
- clean handoff to existing auditors/counsel rather than displacing them;
- continuous re-screening after the baseline without re-performing a full manual desk review.

## Beachhead decision impact

TTO/research licensing remains the strongest evidence-backed first beachhead currently found.

Updated internal beachhead score:
- University/research TTO: **88/100** (up from 86) due to direct procurement, portfolio size, disclosed desk/full-audit economics, resource-constrained monitoring evidence and quantified recoveries.

This score is an internal prioritization score, not a probability of commercial success.

Remaining commercial unknowns:
1. procurement cycle for a low-cost software/service scan versus formal audit RFP;
2. information-security review burden for confidential agreements and sales data;
3. whether TTOs prefer auditor-led triage bundled into incumbent engagements;
4. acceptable fixed-fee entry price for portfolio screening;
5. whether partner auditors will view the product as leverage or channel conflict;
6. whether RoyaltyRecover can empirically drive screening cost far below the ~$3k desk-audit benchmark without degrading evidence quality.
