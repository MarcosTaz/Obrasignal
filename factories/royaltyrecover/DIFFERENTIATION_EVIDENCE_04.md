# RoyaltyRecover — Differentiation Evidence 04

Date: 2026-08-29
Status: GATE 3 EVIDENCE UPDATE — NOT YET PASS

## Current commercial benchmark

The strongest public benchmark remains the University of California proposed royalty-audit identification approach:
- desk audits average approximately **US$3,000 per license**;
- approximately **50%** of desk audits identify concerns meriting transition to a full royalty audit;
- full audits average approximately **US$40,000–US$60,000**;
- the proposed UC model explicitly favors centralized risk-based screening of a normalized portfolio.

Source:
- https://regents.universityofcalifornia.edu/regmeet/july23/s1attach1.pdf

Stout describes desk audits as computational/analytical reviews of reports, payments, public financial information, reporting changes, new products and territories and says a desk audit can typically be executed in **two to three weeks**.

Source:
- https://www.stout.com/en/services/regulatory-compliance/royalty-audit-services

Miller Kaplan royalty-audit commentary gives another service benchmark: desk audits can start around **US$5,000**, while an average audit is cited around **US$25,000–US$30,000** and approximately **six months**.

Source:
- https://imclicensing.com/the-rules-of-royalty-accounting-vincent-leoni-miller-kaplan/

AUTM's September 24, 2026 Royalty Audits 101 program independently describes the expected workflow in the same order RoyaltyRecover is implementing: risk-rate the licensee population -> select licensees -> desk review agreement/reports/payment history -> escalate to a full audit with licensor/auditor/counsel roles -> collect and improve controls.

Source:
- https://autm.net/careers-and-courses/webinars/live-webinars

## Differentiation consequence

Workflow shape alone is **not differentiated**. The market already knows and sells this process.

The surviving product test is economic and operational:

**RoyaltyRecover must turn portfolio-wide first-pass screening into a much cheaper, faster and more reproducible operation than buying a US$3k–US$5k desk audit for every individual license.**

For a 170-license portfolio, applying UC's US$3k average desk-audit cost to every license would imply roughly US$510k of desk-audit spend before any full audits. That multiplication is illustrative only; UC does not say all 170 licenses should be desk-audited.

Therefore the product should be sold as:

`PORTFOLIO BASELINE -> EVIDENCE GAPS -> DETERMINISTIC RECONCILIATION -> DEADLINE/RISK TRIAGE -> SMALL AUDIT CANDIDATE SET -> AUDITOR HANDOFF`

not as:

`CHEAPER ROYALTY AUDITOR`.

## Minimum Gate 3 pass condition

Gate 3 should not pass merely because the software works. Require all of the following:

1. A realistic 100+ agreement portfolio can be screened without per-license consulting effort scaling linearly.
2. Clean/no-recovery cases remain visible and are not forced into audit candidates.
3. Missing evidence and ambiguous contract cases fail closed.
4. Human-reviewed clause/rule lineage is preserved into every persisted monetary candidate.
5. The system produces an auditor-ready candidate set and evidence map rather than claiming to replace contract-required auditors.
6. A bounded pilot price can be set materially below the cost of desk-auditing the same portfolio license-by-license.
7. A partner-auditor handoff model can be defined without channel conflict or invented rights.

## Current verdict

GATE 3: **OPEN, STRONGER**.

The product now has a defensible technical angle — deterministic source lineage, stale-run invalidation, tenant isolation, explicit non-claim state, and portfolio register — but there is not yet market evidence that buyers will pay for this layer separately from incumbent auditors or royalty-management platforms.
