Date: 2026-08-29

## Evidence supporting university / research technology-transfer offices

AUTM's Licensing Activity Survey covers more than 230 U.S. and Canadian research institutions and is the sector's core benchmarking dataset. AUTM's FY2024 U.S. findings report licensing staff declined by more than 4% while the innovation pipeline remained active. FY2025 reports licenses increased more than 7% and nearly 2,000 license amendments were reported. Historical AUTM data also showed technology-transfer offices managing a substantial number of active agreements per office FTE.

## Candidate beachheads

### A. University / research TTOs

Strengths:
- concentrated buyer community and distribution channel through AUTM ecosystem;
- patent/technology licenses are core business;
- active agreements and amendments create version/control workload;
- audit workflow explicitly recognized by sector training;
- audit rights frequently appear in public university/technology-license templates;
- potential for a portfolio-level scan rather than single-dispute consulting.

Weaknesses:
- institutional procurement/security reviews may slow sales;
- many institutions have internal counsel/accounting and existing audit-firm relationships;
- budgets vary significantly;
- market is concentrated but not enormous.

Provisional score: 82/100 for problem/evidence/channel fit; commercial-speed discount still unmeasured.

### B. Mid-market corporate technology/IP licensors

Strengths:
- potentially faster buying decisions;
- high royalty density;
- clearer direct ROI;
- international expansion potential.

Weaknesses:
- difficult to identify externally who has enough running-royalty exposure;
- Connor, Stout, Invotex and other incumbents already serve technology licensors;
- public evidence on buyer budget/deployment pain is weaker than for TTO workflow.

Provisional score: 78/100.

### C. Industrial/OEM embedded-technology licensors

Strengths:
- high-value per-unit and product-classification risk;
- SKU, geography, related-party and acquired-product leakage can be economically material;
- less entertainment-specific infrastructure dependency.

Weaknesses:
- data access can require licensee books/BOM/product mapping;
- strong incumbent audit expertise exists;
- quantified public recovery evidence for this narrower segment needs more work.

Provisional score: 75/100.

### D. Life-science / biotech licensors

Strengths:
- very high value per agreement;
- sublicense revenue, milestones, product scope and complex deductions create dense economics;
- public audit cases show meaningful omission risks.

Weaknesses:
- highest technical/legal complexity;
- long sales cycles and professional-review requirements;
- strong specialist competition.

Provisional score: 74/100 as first beachhead, potentially much higher as a later specialist lane.

## Current decision

**Primary beachhead to validate next: university/research TTO recoverability triage.**

This is a go-to-market hypothesis, not a permanent product restriction. It is selected because problem workflow, buyer concentration and portfolio structure are unusually observable. Gate 2 remains provisional until commercial-speed and incumbent-adoption evidence is strong enough.

## Qualification hypothesis

Do not hard-code a revenue cutoff yet. For TTOs, qualify based on:
- number of active running-royalty agreements;
- material royalty income concentration;
- amendment/side-letter burden;
- audit-right presence;
- last audit date / never-audited licensees;
- incomplete transaction-level verification;
- approaching contractual lookback windows;
- available statements/payment history;
- willingness to use an external qualified auditor when needed.

## Distribution hypothesis

The concentrated route is likely not broad paid advertising. Test:
1. royalty-audit/accounting firms as partner channel;
2. IP/licensing counsel as partner channel;
3. AUTM education/events/direct outreach to TTO finance/compliance/licensing leaders;
4. technology-transfer associations outside North America after initial proof.

The partner proposition remains: RoyaltyRecover triages and prepares evidence; trusted professionals keep the formal audit/legal relationship.
