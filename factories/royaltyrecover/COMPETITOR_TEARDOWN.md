A broad claim of "automated royalty leakage detection + continuous monitoring" is not defensible as a unique wedge. A second claim of "patent / technology royalty recoverability assurance" is also too broad: Connor Consulting explicitly serves semiconductor IP providers, patent pools, technology-transfer teams and technology licensors with royalty audits, licensee risk assessments and data-driven desk reviews, and it promotes tiered compliance programs focused on cost/benefit.

## Revised candidate wedge

**MID-MARKET ROYALTY RECOVERABILITY DESK FOR PATENT / TECHNOLOGY LICENSORS**

Target hypothesis, still to validate:
- multiple running-royalty licensees;
- enough value to justify verification but too little operational scale to justify an enterprise royalty-management migration or permanent specialist compliance team;
- working hypothesis: 3–30 material licensees and roughly EUR 1m–25m annual royalty receipts;
- existing agreements/statements/payments remain source systems;
- zero-migration rapid portfolio screen before a formal audit;
- audit-right/lookback deadline queue;
- deterministic recomputation where source evidence supports it;
- explicit UNKNOWN / NEEDS_DATA / PROFESSIONAL_REVIEW states;
- auditor-ready evidence + information-request pack;
- qualified auditor/counsel escalation when required by contract or law;
- recovery ledger closes only on verified incremental cash received.

The numeric ICP bands above are assumptions, not evidence-backed thresholds yet.

## Why this may still work

AUTM's 2026 Royalty Audits 101 workflow explicitly teaches risk-rating a technology-transfer licensee population, desk-reviewing agreements/reports/payment history, and escalating selected licensees to full audits. That validates the operating sequence but also proves it is not novel. The product opportunity is to make that workflow cheap, deterministic, always-on and accessible to smaller portfolios without pretending to replace the formal auditor.

Connor's published tier model is an important red-team result: it specifically targets mid-tier licensees. Therefore "mid-market" by itself is NOT sufficient differentiation. RoyaltyRecover must prove either materially lower deployment friction/cost, a defensible deadline-first recoverability workflow, or a distribution advantage through audit/IP-law partners.

## Pricing truth

Published market evidence currently supports pricing *forms*, not the Factory's proposed absolute numbers:
- S&W: fixed cost or percentage of audit findings.
- 360 Royalty Audit: fixed fee, or at its discretion fully contingent.
- Acumon: fixed-fee royalty examination.

There is not yet sufficient public evidence here to validate EUR 5k–20k as a market-standard full-audit price. Treat that band as HYPOTHESIS.
