# ObraSignal — Product Superiority Program

**Draft strategic execution contract — do not treat as canonical until merged.**

## Category

ObraSignal should compete as a **Construction Commercial Intelligence System**, not as a generic tender-alert product, generic procurement suite, ERP, CRM, marketplace, or bid-management clone.

The governing product question is:

> **Where should this contractor compete, when should it move, what deserves bid resources, what blocks execution, and what is the best next economically defensible action?**

The product must become memorable because it improves decisions, timing and execution readiness — not because it has the longest feature list.

## Three superiority pillars

### 1. Decision superiority

The customer should receive a small set of economically meaningful decisions rather than a large ranked list.

Target decision states:
- **PURSUE** — strong evidence that the opportunity deserves active bid resources;
- **REVIEW** — potentially attractive, but material uncertainty or blocker remains;
- **WATCH** — strategically relevant but not yet actionable;
- **PASS** — available evidence does not justify bid resources.

No opaque single score may substitute for the decision explanation.

Every material decision should progressively separate:
- company fit;
- commercial attractiveness;
- competitive position;
- effort;
- risk;
- timing;
- evidence quality;
- unresolved blockers.

### 2. Timing superiority

ObraSignal should progressively detect commercially useful demand **before formal tender publication** where legal and source-quality evidence supports it.

Candidate signal families include:
- procurement plans;
- prior-information notices;
- budgets and capital programmes;
- framework/contract expiries;
- planning/permit signals;
- buyer cycles;
- previous awards;
- market-dialogue and forecast signals.

The target metric is **Lead Time Advantage**: how much earlier ObraSignal produces an actionable, evidence-backed signal than the formal procurement event.

### 3. Execution superiority

A strong opportunity is not enough if the contractor cannot execute it.

After a PURSUE/REVIEW decision, ObraSignal should progressively determine:
- native execution capacity;
- missing qualifications/certifications;
- workforce/capacity constraints;
- equipment constraints;
- key supply requirements;
- subcontracting gaps;
- guarantee/insurance requirements;
- capital/cash exposure;
- mobilisation/logistics constraints;
- hard blockers versus economically unlockable blockers.

The product must not become a visible affiliate marketplace. Partner/supplier/finance/equipment options should appear only when they resolve a real opportunity-specific blocker and must remain commercially independent from ranking.

## Core product graphs

Long-term differentiation should compound through linked, provenance-aware graphs:

1. **Contractor Graph** — capabilities, constraints, preferences, observed decisions and outcomes.
2. **Opportunity Graph** — tenders plus pre-tender and post-award signals, lifecycle and change history.
3. **Buyer Graph** — spend, procurement patterns, award history, contract cycles and incumbency.
4. **Competitor Graph** — awards, buyers, geographies, categories and trajectory.
5. **Temporal Graph** — when facts became true, when they were observed, what changed, and expected cycles.
6. **Supply/Capacity Graph** — only after repeated real demand proves value; suppliers, equipment, specialist capacity and other execution resources.

Decision logic should consume these graphs rather than devolve into `tender × keywords → score`.

## Contractor Graph: minimum useful evolution

The company profile must evolve beyond static CPV/keyword matching. Progressively model only decision-relevant facts such as:
- trades/specialties;
- geographies and travel radius;
- project-value comfort bands;
- contract/procedure preferences;
- certifications and qualifications;
- known exclusions;
- historical PURSUE/REVIEW/WATCH/PASS decisions;
- known wins/losses/no-bids where available;
- buyer preferences;
- execution constraints;
- material capacity facts where consented and useful.

Do not build a generic HR/ERP system to collect this data. Integrate or capture only what materially improves decisions.

## Decision Brief UX

The default customer experience should compress complexity.

A strong home surface should answer:
- **What needs attention today?**
- **What changed materially?**
- **What is forming next?**
- **Which buyers/competitors require attention?**
- **What execution blockers can be resolved?**

The customer should not need to interpret internal scoring, lineage, source-health or confidence machinery to know what to do next.

Desired shape:

- a small number of PURSUE/REVIEW decisions;
- material changes since last visit;
- early signals;
- buyer/competitor movements;
- blockers and recommended next action;
- evidence and uncertainty on demand.

## Product-superiority metrics

The programme must optimise for real customer decision quality rather than activity volume.

Primary candidate metrics:

### Attention quality
- **Useful Opportunities per 10 Reviewed**;
- false-positive rate among top-ranked items;
- time spent per useful decision.

### Decision quality
- Precision@5;
- Precision@10;
- missed-relevant rate;
- disagreement rate between recommendation and later expert/customer evidence;
- decision reversals caused by previously missing material evidence.

### Timing
- Lead Time Advantage;
- fraction of useful signals detected before formal publication;
- early-signal false-positive/expiry rate.

### Customer action
- time from signal to qualified decision;
- time from decision to first meaningful action;
- PURSUE/REVIEW actions taken;
- repeat use of decision brief.

### Execution readiness
- opportunities with correctly identified material blockers;
- blockers resolved;
- opportunities unlocked where economics remain defensible;
- mobilisation time/cost reduced where causally defensible.

### Retention/value
- repeat-value frequency;
- decision-support usage across cohorts;
- retained paid usage;
- measured versus estimated value must remain explicitly separated.

No metric may be promoted without a defined denominator and evidence source.

## Benchmark contract

ObraSignal must prove superiority, not infer it from feature parity.

Build a progressively stronger benchmark using real opportunities and real contractor contexts where legally and operationally permitted.

Compare against appropriate baselines:
- official portal/manual discovery;
- keyword/CPV baseline;
- current ObraSignal rules;
- stronger internal candidate;
- competitor output only where lawful and practically comparable.

Measure at minimum:
- Precision@5 / Precision@10;
- false positives;
- missed relevant opportunities;
- time-to-decision;
- explanation fidelity;
- tenant/company specificity.

Synthetic corpora prove invariants. They do not prove market superiority.

## Buyer Intelligence

For a buyer, ObraSignal should progressively answer:
- what does this buyer buy?
- how often?
- through what procedures/frameworks?
- typical values/lots?
- who wins?
- who appears repeatedly?
- what contracts/frameworks are approaching a likely new cycle?
- what planned demand is emerging?

Buyer intelligence should improve decision quality, timing or account strategy. Avoid decorative analytics.

## Competitor Intelligence

Progressively model evidence-backed competitor patterns:
- award history;
- buyers;
- categories/trades;
- geographies;
- contract values;
- recurring competitive overlap;
- recent material movements.

Do not imply confidential knowledge, win probability or causation without evidence.

## Change Intelligence

An opportunity already seen by the customer should become more valuable when ObraSignal detects what changed.

Track material changes such as:
- deadline;
- scope;
- lots;
- value;
- documents;
- lifecycle;
- buyer clarification/amendment;
- eligibility/decision implication.

When the recommendation changes, explain **what evidence changed and why the decision changed**.

## MOBILIZE — execution readiness

MOBILIZE is not a generic marketplace. It is an opportunity-specific capacity-gap and mobilisation layer.

Potential flow:

`PURSUE/REVIEW → requirements → current capacity → blockers → unlockability → economic options → mobilisation plan`

A blocker can be:
- NATIVE / already satisfied;
- UNLOCKABLE / resolvable with evidence-backed lawful option;
- HARD / not rationally resolvable;
- UNKNOWN / insufficient evidence.

Examples of unlock domains, only when real demand proves value:
- specialist subcontracting;
- equipment rental/acquisition;
- critical supply;
- logistics/accommodation;
- guarantees/insurance via authorised partners;
- financing via authorised partners.

No partner payment may change the decision, blocker classification or ranking.

## Economic pursuit and portfolio allocation

ObraSignal should not optimise bids one by one when resources are shared.

Progressively evaluate:
- bid preparation effort;
- mobilisation cost;
- capacity displacement;
- cash exposure;
- buyer strategic value;
- execution risk;
- opportunity interaction/overlap.

The mature question is:

> **Given limited bid and execution capacity, which combination of opportunities should this contractor pursue?**

Do not present fabricated expected-profit or win-probability precision.

## Three customer horizons

### NOW
Decisions requiring attention today.

### NEXT
Emerging opportunities, changes, contract/framework cycles and early signals.

### POSITION
Buyers, markets, capabilities or capacity where strategic preparation may create future advantage.

This keeps ObraSignal useful even when no new formal tender requires action today.

## International strategy

Country count is not the product moat.

Optimise for **sellable market depth**:
- legal/reuse clarity;
- official source quality;
- freshness/completeness;
- national/below-threshold depth where valuable;
- local procurement semantics;
- localisation;
- buyer/competitor history;
- commercial access;
- maintainability.

Shared connector families should maximise scale without pretending shared transport implies full local coverage.

## Monetisation doctrine

Revenue must follow customer value.

Sequence:
1. recurring SaaS for recurring decision value;
2. premium intelligence/team/multi-market value where proven;
3. transaction facilitation only when real repeated customer demand exists;
4. partner/referral economics only through independent, transparent ranking;
5. network/platform revenue only after product-led interaction density exists.

Never manufacture a marketplace before repeated opportunity-specific demand exists.

## Distribution doctrine

Engineering superiority without distribution is not company superiority.

Continuously test scalable access through:
- construction/trade associations;
- procurement/bid consultants;
- ERP/accounting/preconstruction software ecosystems;
- insurers/guarantee/equipment networks where aligned;
- referrals;
- targeted direct sales;
- country-specific channel partners.

Prioritise channels that produce qualified users and retained value, not vanity reach.

## Explicit anti-goals

Do not compete by rebuilding mature generic categories unless they materially strengthen decision advantage:
- generic CRM;
- generic ERP;
- accounting/payroll;
- deep project management;
- generic document management;
- chat/email clients;
- broad supplier marketplace;
- full bid-management suite merely for parity.

Integrate where possible.

## Execution order

Do not jump straight to platform/network features.

### Phase 1 — Decision superiority
- Contractor Graph minimum useful model;
- decision decomposition;
- PURSUE / REVIEW / WATCH / PASS semantics;
- reason fidelity;
- real Top-K benchmark;
- Decision Brief UX.

### Phase 2 — Context moat
- Buyer Graph;
- Competitor Graph;
- award/contract history;
- opportunity change intelligence.

### Phase 3 — Timing moat
- procurement plans/PINs;
- budgets/capital plans;
- framework/contract cycles;
- early-signal benchmark and Lead Time Advantage.

### Phase 4 — Execution moat
- execution-readiness model;
- capacity-gap classification;
- unlock research;
- mobilisation planning.

### Phase 5 — Economic network
- selective RFQ/supplier/equipment/finance/insurance rails only when repeated real demand is proven;
- Capacity/Supply Graph;
- independent recommendation economics.

### Phase 6 — Strategic operating system
- portfolio allocation;
- market-entry intelligence;
- forward-capacity strategy;
- Economic Capability Graph.

Promotion between phases is evidence-gated, not calendar-gated.

## Priority test

When work competes for scarce execution capacity, prefer the item that most improves the product's shortest path to one of these outcomes:

1. **Better Selection**
2. **Earlier Timing**
3. **Better Context**
4. **Better Decisions**
5. **Execution Readiness**
6. **Repeat Value / Retention**

Cross-tenant/security/privacy/data-loss/customer-truth P0s remain non-negotiable safety overrides.

## Permanent superiority test

For every material product capability ask:

> **If the same contractor and the same market were given to ObraSignal and the strongest credible alternative, would the contractor make a better, faster, more economically defensible decision with ObraSignal?**

If the answer cannot eventually be demonstrated, the capability is not a strategic moat merely because it is technically sophisticated.

## Internal strategic line

> **Know where to compete. Move before the market. Be ready to deliver.**

This is a product direction, not permission to overclaim customer outcomes.
